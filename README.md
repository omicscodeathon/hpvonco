# hpvonco
In-silico Analysis of Phytochemicals Targeting HPV-16 and HPV-18 Oncoproteins for HPV-related Cancer Management
# Overview

Human papillomavirus (HPV) infections are the primary causative agents of various types of cancers, including cervical, anal, penile, and oropharyngeal cancers. These infections are highly prevalent, with over 84.6% and 91.3% sexually active women and men respectively are expected to contract HPV at some point in their lives. Among the various types of HPV, HPV-16 and HPV-18 are particularly notorious, being high-risk subtypes responsible for approximately over 79% of cervical cancer. Current treatments for HPV-associated conditions include a variety of methods but these treatments often result in recurrence and can be painful and expensive. Due to this, there is a critical need to explore alternative therapeutic approaches that can effectively target the HPV oncoproteins and contribute to the management of HPV-related cancers in a more efficient and less harmful manner. _Allium sativum_, commonly known as garlic, has been used in traditional medicine for centuries due to its wide range of therapeutic properties, including antimicrobial, antiviral, and anti-inflammatory effects. Various studies have demonstrated that garlic possesses antiviral activity against HPV, but the specific bioactive compounds within garlic that exert this antiviral effect, particularly against HPV-16 and HPV-18 oncoproteins, have not been thoroughly investigated. In this study, we utilized molecular modelling approaches - Molecular Docking, Lipinski's Ro5, Pharmacokinetics analysis, Density functional theory, and Molecular Dynamic Simulation - to examine the inhibitory characteristics of the bioactive compouds derived from Allium sativum on the HPV-16 and HPV-18 Oncoproteins.

# Objective
The main objective of this study is to identify and assess the phytochemicals derived from _allium sativum_ in inhibiting HPV-16 and HPV-18 oncoproteins using in-silico approaches.

# Methodology
Protein Structure Acquisition and Homology Modeling
The three-dimensional structures of E6 oncoproteins from HPV 16 (resolution: 2.25 Å) and HPV 18 (resolution: 2.03 Å) were retrieved from the RCSB PDB database (https://www.rcsb.org/) using the PDB IDs 4XR8 and 6SJV, respectively, in PDB format. As the structures of E7 oncoproteins for both HPV 16 and HPV 18 have not yet been established, homology modeling was conducted. The query sequences of E7 oncoproteins for HPV 16 and HPV 18 were retrieved from the NCBI database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) in FASTA format using accession IDs NP_041326.1 and NP_040311.1, respectively. Template structures for homology modeling were identified through a BLAST search by comparing the protein sequences against the Protein Data Bank (PDB) to find homologous structures. The results were assessed based on sequence similarity, query coverage, and E-value, leading to the selection of the most appropriate templates. The identified template structures were downloaded from the RCSB PDB in PDB format. Homology modeling of the E7 proteins from HPV 16 and HPV 18 was performed using SWISS-MODEL (https://swissmodel.expasy.org/) (Guex et al., 2009; Waterhouse et al., 2018), with PDB IDs 2B9D and 6IWD selected as templates. The final modeled structures were visualized using Chimera software, Version 1.8.
Protein Structure Validation
To ensure the quality of the homology models, a series of validation steps were performed. The modeled proteins were validated using PROCHECK to assess stereochemical quality through Ramachandran plot analysis (Laskowski et al., 1993) and ERRAT for overall model quality (Colovos & Yeates, 1993). These validations were conducted using the SAVES 6.1 (Structure Analysis and Validation Server) web server (https://saves.mbi.ucla.edu/). Additionally, the ProSA-web server (https://prosa.services.came.sbg.ac.at/) was used to validate and calculate Z-scores for residue energies in the modeled proteins (Sippl, 1993; Wiederstein & Sippl, 2007).
Phytochemicals Retrieval and Screening
Sample Preparation
A total of 74 phytochemicals from Allium sativum, known for their antioxidant properties, anticancer effects, and antiviral activities, were retrieved from the Indian Medicinal Plants, Phytochemistry and Therapeutics (IMPPAT) database (https://cb.imsc.res.in/imppat/home) (Mohanraj et al., 2018; Vivek-Ananth et al., 2023) and the COCONUT database (https://coconut.naturalproducts.net/).
Drug-Likeness Analysis
The retrieved phytochemicals were screened for drug-likeness using the Lipinski Rule of Five via the SCFBIO web server (https://scfbio-iitd.res.in/software/drugdesign/lipinski.jsp#anchortag). Compounds were considered drug-like if they did not violate more than two of the following criteria: molecular mass ≤ 500 Daltons, lipophilicity (LogP) ≤ 5, hydrogen bond donors (HBD) ≤ 5, hydrogen bond acceptors (HBA) ≤ 10, and molar refractivity (MR) between 40 and 130 (Jayaram et al., 2012; Lipinski, 2004)
Antiviral Activity Prediction
The phytochemicals were further screened for antiviral activity using the PASS web server (http://way2drug.com/PassOnline/). A Pa (Potential Activity) score > 0.3 (medium confidence) was used as the threshold for selecting phytochemicals for molecular docking. Additionally, the Pi (Probability of Inactivity) score had to be lower than the Pa score for the compound to be considered effective in the human body (Poroikov et al., 2019). 44 phytochemicals having drug-like properties and antiviral probability above 0.3 were selected for molecular docking study.
